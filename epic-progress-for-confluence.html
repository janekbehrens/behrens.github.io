Epic progress, live on any page

Add Epic Progress from the / menu and pick the epic. The bar shows what is done, in progress and to do, colored like in Jira.

  • Count work items or story points, with “4 of 8 work items done” next to every percent.
  • Status, due date, flagged work and up to 25 open work items.
  • Every reader sees the numbers their own Jira permissions allow.

Releases, filters and lists of epics

Jira Progress shows a project, a release (fix version), a saved filter or a JQL search. Progress List shows several epics with an overall row.

  • All epics of a project, the epics of an initiative or your own pick.
  • Least progress or earliest due date first.
  • Release dates, in red when an unreleased version is past its date.
More in the docs →

Status reports for readers without Jira

An editor with Jira access publishes a dated snapshot with one click. Everyone who can see the page sees it, also without Jira.

  • Editors’ visits keep its numbers current, at most every 15 minutes, with the editor’s own Jira permissions.
  • Reading a snapshot never causes a Jira request.
  • No assignees or account IDs. Editors can remove it at any time; admins can turn snapshots off.

Settings with a live preview

Type /progress and pick one of five macros. Every field has a short explanation, and the preview shows the macro with live data before you save.

  • Search pickers for epics, projects, releases, filters and spaces.
  • Save checks the settings first and names what is missing.
More in the docs →

No Jira needed: labels and manual bars

Page Progress counts the pages that carry a label such as “approved”. Progress Bar is set by hand, for work that is not tracked in Jira.

  • Pages below a chosen page, in this space or in another space.
  • Status colors you choose: on track, at risk, off track.
  • “Updated 4 Oct 2026” shows how fresh a hand-set value is.

FAQ

Common questions

More answers in the documentation, or write to support@janekbehrens.de.

How do I show Jira epic progress on a Confluence page?
Edit a page, type /progress and choose Epic Progress. Search the epic by name or key, check the live preview and save. The bar shows how many of the epic's work items are done, in progress and to do, from their status in Jira, like Jira's own child-issue progress.
Can people without Jira access see the progress?
Yes, with a published snapshot. An editor of the page who can see the work items in Jira clicks Publish snapshot, and everyone who can see the page then sees these numbers with their date. When an editor opens the page and the snapshot is older than 15 minutes, its numbers are read again with that editor's Jira permissions; this can be turned off per macro. Reading a snapshot never causes a Jira request. A snapshot keeps no assignees or account IDs.
Who sees which numbers?
Every reader sees the numbers their own Jira permissions allow. The app reads Jira and Confluence with the permissions of the person reading the page, so issue security and page restrictions apply. People without access to the work items see a short note instead of numbers, unless an editor published a snapshot.
Do I need Jira?
No. Progress Bar and Page Progress work with Confluence alone. Epic Progress, Jira Progress and Progress List read Jira on the same site; a site admin connects Jira in Atlassian Administration after the app is installed in Confluence.
Does the app change anything in Jira or Confluence?
No. It has no write access to Jira or Confluence. Besides published snapshots, it keeps only a little app data without personal data in Atlassian's Forge storage, and it runs on Atlassian with no external servers.
Is it free?
Confluence sites with up to 10 users pay nothing, with all five macros and snapshots. Larger sites start with a free trial; after the trial it is priced per user.
